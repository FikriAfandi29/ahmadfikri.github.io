Research Projects CV

I am an undergraduate student in Development Economics at Sultan Ageng Tirtayasa University. My research focuses on monetary economics, macroeconomics, and empirical asset pricing.

My current work uses granular institutional holdings data to study how offsetting portfolio adjustments between active and passive managers shape aggregate market stability after macro-financial shocks. Other related work examines how fiscal conditions and debt maturity shape U.S. inflation dynamics following monetary tightening under equilibrium-selection frameworks. In a second research agenda, I apply large language models to central bank communications to measure inflation narratives and asymmetric policy-rate responses.

Equilibrium Selection, Debt Maturity, and U.S. Inflation Dynamics
Paper Link Replication Code
Can an equilibrium-selection framework better explain U.S. inflation dynamics following monetary tightening than a traditional Taylor rule, and how do fiscal conditions shape this response? Using U.S. quarterly data from 1990 to 2025 and high-frequency identified monetary policy shocks, this study estimates impulse responses through local projections and evaluates the analytical inflation path implied by Cochrane's equilibrium-selection framework against a traditional Taylor rule with autoregressive disturbances. The results reveal a short-run price puzzle and show that the equilibrium-selection framework better reproduces the initial inflation response. Further analysis identifies complementary fiscal channels: stronger primary surpluses are associated with a lower immediate inflation response, whereas a higher share of long-term government debt is associated with stronger disinflation over the medium term. These findings highlight the importance of fiscal conditions in monetary policy transmission.

Publications

    Forecasting Indonesia’s Inflation Using Machine Learning: A Policy-Oriented Comparison with Traditional Models
    Forthcoming, Bulletin of Monetary Economics and Banking
    Paper Link
    The aim of the paper is to evaluate the predictive precision of deep learning architectures against robust econometric frameworks for forecasting Indonesia's inflation. By using the Narayan and Popp (2010) structural break test and the Westerlund and Narayan (WN) framework, the study finds that the Multivariate LSTM model significantly outperforms traditional ARIMA, ARIMAX, and robust linear benchmarks. Further, the results also confirm the LSTM's unique ability to internalize non-linear "pass-through" effects from global energy shocks. Thus, the implementation of this autonomous early-warning system provides critical insights for Bank Indonesia in calibrating monetary policy amidst macroeconomic instability.

  • [2]
    Aggregate Inertia and Heterogeneous Institutional Rebalancing: Evidence from Macro-Financial Shocks
    Under Review, Bulletin of Monetary Economics and Banking
    We study how institutional investors rebalance equity portfolios after macro-financial shocks. Using granular holdings data at the manager–stock–quarter level from 2015Q1 to 2025Q4, we estimate dynamic responses of returns and portfolio-weight changes to shifts in the Federal Funds Rate, economic policy uncertainty, and market volatility. While stock returns respond strongly, aggregate portfolio weights show little adjustment. This apparent stability masks a systematic offsetting-rebalancing mechanism beneath the aggregate surface, where active and passive managers adjust their portfolios in opposite directions, leaving the net aggregate quantity response statistically indistinguishable from zero. Our results point to an aggregate illusion, demonstrating that macro-level market stability coexists with and is inherently insulated by heterogeneous portfolio adjustments across competing institutional investor mandates.

Works in Progress

  • [1]
    Inside Bank Indonesia: Inflation Narratives, Beliefs, and Asymmetric Policy Responses
    with Cep Jandi Anwar
    Does Bank Indonesia respond symmetrically to demand- and supply-related inflation pressures? We analyze 233 monetary policy documents from November 2005 to September 2026 to construct monthly measures of policymakers' economic assessments using a two-stage large language model framework. We find no statistically significant asymmetry between demand and supply narratives in the full sample. However, following the August 2016 introduction of the BI 7-Day Reverse Repo Rate, monetary policy responses become asymmetric: demand narratives are positively and significantly associated with policy-rate adjustments, while supply narratives remain insignificant. Inflation-expectations narratives also become significantly associated with policy-rate adjustments during this period. These findings remain robust across alternative specifications, breakpoint tests, and wild bootstrap inference.
